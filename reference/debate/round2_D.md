# Path D: round 2

## Rebuttal

Three briefs make the strongest objection: D's first year runs on priors, and a new anonymous site will not reach the rating density the method needs (Fable; A; B). I concede it. My own numbers say the same: at 2,000 people, about 18 perfumes reach the 120 raters needed to test one disputed drydown family (round1_D.md, claim). As a source of truth, D gives users nothing in year one that A does not already give them.

I concede two further objections in part.

**Anosmia is larger than my brief assumed.** Scent and Chemistry reports that 29.5% of women and 37.2% of men could not smell androstenone in a survey of 1.5 million people, and "a similar number" for Galaxolide (reference/books/scent_and_chemistry_2022.txt line 1992). In my simulation, 30% non-smellers cut detection power at 120 raters from 0.87 to 0.64, so musks are the hardest family for D to test. The same fact weakens every tag: a white_musk weight of 0.6 describes a smell that about a third of wearers may not perceive. Only per-person data can tell the profiler that a given user need not avoid Galaxolide.

**Co-occurring materials (C).** Ratings cannot say which of Iso E Super, Galaxolide or vanillin a person rejects when the three appear together. That is true. Separating them needs perfumes that contain one without the others, and C's own label test supplies one: Kalan declares no Iso E Super (round1_C.md, evidence 2). The site already asks users to try exactly such a perfume (README.md lines 75 to 76). Ratings cannot name materials; labels and books name them, and ratings test whether the named material predicts dislike.

**What survives.** A sends "consistent stage-level contradiction" to a review queue (round1_A.md, claim) but does not say how much contradiction counts. D supplies the threshold: about 120 raters for a 1.5-point drydown drop among 15% of raters, about 200 for a 1-point drop, at a false-positive rate near 5%. That makes D a component of A, not a rival source of truth, and I vote accordingly.

## Vote

1. **A (provenance stack).** It launches in weeks on the audited tags, shows users which claims are marketing, gains label evidence for Iso E Super and Galaxolide as new-format lists spread, and needs about 40 hours and no new money.
2. **B (vendor and crowd first).** It reaches the Arab-house perfumes Saudi users own fastest and most cheaply, but its own test shows the mapper reproduces only 8 of the 69 book corrections, so it gives up the product's thesis.
3. **D (data first, my path).** Its statistics are the right way to use ratings, but for a year it changes nothing users see and at realistic traffic tests about 20 perfumes, so it belongs inside A as the review threshold.
4. **C (materials first).** Its label evidence is the strongest per entry (8 of 12 Parfums de Marly entries contradicted), but leaving most of the catalogue and nearly all Arab houses untagged fails the launch-in-weeks and Saudi-audience constraints.
