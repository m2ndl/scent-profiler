# Round 2, Path B

## Rebuttal

The strongest objection comes from C (evidence item 2), and A, D and Fable make the same point: pyramids leave out the leading material, B cannot see it, and users cannot flag a material nobody told them about. I checked it on the brand's own pages (parfums-de-marly.com/products/layton, /althair, /kalan, fetched 25 September 2026). Layton declares Tetramethyl Acetyloctahydronaphthalenes (Iso E Super) as the first material after water, plus patchouli oil and coumarin; our entry has no cedar_dry, patchouli or tonka_coumarin (data.js lines 106 to 110). Althaïr lists Iso E Super, vanillin and Galaxolide before water and declares no coumarin; our entry carries tonka_coumarin 0.6 and white_musk only 0.3 (data.js lines 116 to 120). Kalan declares no Iso E Super, so the lists discriminate. My own round 1 test agrees: mapping notes reproduced 8 of 69 audit corrections.

I concede three points. Pure B ships errors that a label on the same product page would fix, for EU-sold niche and designer perfumes. "Correct what users flag" cannot catch a family the page never names. For a site whose thesis is that notes are not ingredients, that is a real cost, not a detail.

What I keep. The objection shows that labels should be added to B, not that notes should be removed. Every path that launches for this audience needs a notes floor: none of the 42 Arab-house originals has book evidence, Golden Scent shows no ingredient list for Khamrah, and Open Beauty Facts has 208 perfumes with any ingredient text. The gain from labels is also limited. The allergen list omits Ambrox, Cashmeran and Norlimbanol, so woody_amber, the family the site blames first, stays on notes and literature under every path; A's own estimate is that labels would have clearly flagged about 10 of 46 literature-driven corrections. On legality, B as I proposed it uses Fragella only; I rejected FragDB in round 1 for the reasons A gives.

So the defensible form of B is B plus a label overlay: vendor notes for every perfume, and an ingredient list, where one exists, adding or confirming families. That is close to A and to Fable's proposal, at about 15 extra builder hours and no extra money. I therefore do not rank my own path first.

## Vote

1. **A.** It keeps B's notes floor for coverage and adds the label layer whose value I verified on Layton and Althaïr, for about 15 more hours and no extra money, within every constraint in the BRIEF.
2. **B.** It is the fastest and cheapest way to tag what Saudi users own, but it knowingly ships errors that a label on the same page would correct.
3. **D.** It costs little and changes nothing at launch, but for the first year it runs on priors, and its payoff depends on traffic a new site is unlikely to reach.
4. **C.** Its tags are the most reliable, but it leaves all 42 Arab-house originals and most of the catalogue untagged, which fails the Saudi audience and the launch-in-weeks constraint.
