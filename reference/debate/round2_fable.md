# Round 2: Fable

## Rebuttal

The strongest objection to my position came from C and B together: in a provenance stack most entries sit on the lowest layer, and that layer is the one the audit corrected in 40 of 68 checkable cases and the Parfums de Marly label test contradicted in 8 of 12. So, they say, A ships B's tags with extra bookkeeping.

Two answers and one concession.

First, the bookkeeping is the product. B shows the same wrong tag with no grade; A shows the grade. A user who sees "woody ambers: from the brand's note list" and "Iso E Super: declared on the 2026 label, second material" is being told something true about how much to trust each verdict, and the profile engine can weight a notes-only family below a label-backed one. That is not cosmetic: the engine's confidence multiplier already exists (half weight for auto-tagged entries) and simply gains a third value.

Second, the label layer has now been tested on the houses the books ignore, and it caught errors nothing else could. C's own test on twelve Parfums de Marly labels found Iso E Super declared among the first two materials on eleven, absent from every official note list, and mistagged in ten of our entries; Althaïr and Pegasus carry a tonka tag with no coumarin declared. Those are exactly the perfumes Saudi users rate, and the fix cost minutes per entry. B's mechanical mapping reproduced 8 of 69 audit corrections; labels would have caught a further 10 to 20 by A's own estimate, and more each year as the 2026 rule spreads.

The concession: labels cannot see the woody-amber family. Ambrox, Cashmeran and Norlimbanol are not allergens, and Baccarat Rouge 540's own new-format label names none of its three defining materials. For the family the site blames first, the stack is notes plus literature, and the interface must say so on every woody-amber verdict. D's point that ratings are the only source that observes the drydown on skin is also right; A uses them as a review queue and as the held-out test for every other layer, which is where their reliability is sufficient.

## Vote

1. A: the only path that launches in weeks with full coverage and can still show, per tag, what it knows and how.
2. B: the fastest honest fallback if label collection fails A's week-six test, and the coverage floor A stands on in any case.
3. C: the discipline every other path borrows, but alone it leaves most Saudi bottles untagged at launch.
4. D: reactions are not composition, and by its own simulation the first year yields checks on about eighteen perfumes.

Ranking: A, B, C, D.
