# Round 1 brief: Fable (participant, no assigned path)

## Claim
I argue for a provenance stack close to path A, with two changes forced by evidence I gathered today: the ingredient-list layer is stronger than the brief assumed, because Iso E Super and Galaxolide join the mandatory label list in 2026, and the vendor layer should be treated as the floor for coverage, which is path B's real point. Ratings stay on the reaction side and never rewrite tags.

In 3 months: every perfume anyone rates has a mechanical tag set from vendor notes (coverage near 100 percent of what people type), a material overlay for whatever has an ingredient list a wearer or the builder pasted (a few hundred), and quoted literature for about 70. Accuracy at the drydown is marketing level for the floor, material level for the overlay. Cost about $12 a month. In 12 months: labels compliant with the 2026 rule start naming Iso E Super, Galaxolide and Exaltolide, so the overlay covers the two synthetics that cause most drydown complaints, for every new launch, without any expert.

## Evidence
1. The vendor feed has no ingredients. Fragella's fragrance object exposes 25 fields: notes by stage, accords, image, price, longevity, sillage, rankings. No INCI or ingredients field. Source: https://api.fragella.com/docs.html, fetched today.
2. The 2026 allergen list names the drydown synthetics. The 56 substances added by Regulation (EU) 2023/1545 include "Tetramethyl acetyloctahydronaphthalenes" (OTNE, Iso E Super), "Hexamethylindanopyran" (HHCB, Galaxolide), "Hexadecanolactone" (Exaltolide), "Acetyl cedrene", "Rose ketones", santalol, sclareol and vanillin. Ambrox, Cashmeran, Norlimbanol and Habanolide are absent. Source: https://cosmeservice.com/news/regulation2023-1545-changes-the-allergen-list/, fetched today. Deadlines: 31 July 2026 for products newly placed on the market, 31 July 2028 for all (https://coslaw.eu/fragrance-allergens-july-eu-deadline-faq/).
3. Saudi retailers already publish ingredient lists. Faces (faces.sa) product pages carry lists such as "Alcohol Denat., Fragrance (Parfum), Water (Aqua), Ethylhexyl Methoxycinnamate, Butyl Methoxydibenzoylmethane, Ethylhexyl Salicylate, Limonene, Citral, Cinnamal, Linalool, Coumarin" for Rabanne Invictus Victory. Source: https://www.faces.sa/en/p/paco-rabanne-invictus-victory-eau-de-parfum-025415320704.html (seen in search results; the page itself refused an automated fetch, as did Notino and Sephora, which returned 403).
4. Open data does not cover perfume ingredients yet. Open Beauty Facts returns 880 products for "eau de parfum", most without an ingredient text; the few with one are full lists in concentration order. Source: https://world.openbeautyfacts.org/cgi/search.pl?search_terms=eau+de+parfum&json=1, fetched today.
5. Materials, not notes, decide the drydown, and the book gives doses. "Iso E Super (1.45, ca. 35%) in Déclaration" (reference/books/scent_and_chemistry_2022.txt, line 6226); Ambrox is used "in ca. 40% of all perfumes" at trace level (line 5308); Iso E Super "has shaped and defined modern perfumery" (line 3948). The top, heart and base structure the site uses is the industry's own volatility convention (line 1768, the chypre description).
6. Where evidence exists, the marketing-based tags were wrong one time in three. The audit found source evidence for 68 of 286 entries and corrected 40 of them; examples: Le Male's base is 39 percent musk and was untagged; Oud Satin Mood's oud is not perceptible to the Guide's reviewer. Source: reference/audit/tags_part1_summary.md to tags_part4_summary.md and applied_changes.jsonl.
7. Ratings cannot identify materials. Specific anosmia to musks affects about 6 to 9 percent of people (Galaxolide via OR4D6; muscone via OR5AN1). Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36625229/ and https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1009564. A neutral drydown rating from such a person is silence, not tolerance.
8. Scraping the reference sites is not an option. Fragrantica's terms prohibit automated access and unofficial APIs. Source: https://www.fragrantica.com/terms-of-service.phtml.
9. Retailer pages resist automation. Notino, Sephora and Faces all refused automated fetches today (403 or redirect loops), so ingredient lists must be pasted, photographed or entered by hand, not harvested.

## Failure modes and falsifiers
- The "Parfum" line hides everything that is not an allergen. Ambrox, Cashmeran and the newer woody ambers never appear, so the ingredient layer cannot catch the whole woody-amber family. Falsifier: if the 2026 labels turn out to list OTNE and HHCB only rarely (thresholds not met), the layer adds little for drydowns.
- Concentration order is coarse. Only ingredients above 1 percent must be in descending order; allergens are listed when above 0.001 percent, so position tells you little below the top few.
- Reformulations and concentration variants. A list from the EDT may not match the EDP; a list from 2019 may not match today's bottle.
- Entry cost. Without automation, ingredient lists come from the builder and from wearers who paste them; if wearers do not bother, the overlay stays at a few hundred entries.
- Builder time. The material table (each material to a family and a volatility class) is about 150 rows to write carefully; errors there propagate to every entry.

## Weakest point of each rival
- Path B. Its accuracy ceiling is the marketing pyramid, and the only test we have says that ceiling is wrong on one entry in three where it matters. A product whose thesis is "notes are not ingredients" cannot run on notes alone and stay honest. B is right about coverage, which is why the vendor feed should be the floor, not the whole building.
- Path C. Coverage collapses to what has published materials: 68 of the 286 most famous perfumes, and almost none of the Arab houses. A recommender that answers "unknown" for most of what a Saudi wearer owns is not usable at launch, and the evidence pool grows only as fast as one builder can read.
- Path D. Ratings measure reactions, not composition, and the two are confounded by anosmia, skin and vocabulary. Latent-factor models need hundreds of ratings per item to stabilise; a solo site will not reach that for more than a handful of perfumes in a year. Fragrantica's own vote counts, tens of thousands for a bestseller, show the scale required.

Builder hours to a first useful version: material table with families and volatility classes, 6 hours; ingredient-list parser, 4; paste-or-photograph field and provenance display, 4; data model change so each tag records its source, 4. About 18 hours, most of it the material table.
