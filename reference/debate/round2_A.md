# Path A: round 2

## Rebuttal

The strongest objection comes from C, and B and D repeat it. At launch most entries sit on the notes tier, so A serves B's tags to most users and down-weights them by a factor nobody can calibrate. For Arab houses the upper tiers are empty.

**Conceded.** Most entries, and nearly all Arab-house entries, will rest on notes at launch. I checked today: Faces KSA shows an ingredient list for Rabanne Invictus Victory but none for Lattafa Asad. Golden Scent and Niche Gallery show none. For Arab houses, A at launch is B plus a visible "from the brand's notes" label. For the 13 families with no declarable marker, woody_amber among them, the rank-1 weight stays a judgment, and I cannot calibrate it.

**Answered.**
1. The label tier reaches perfumes the books do not. C's own test found new-format lists on 12 official Parfums de Marly pages, all from the audit's no-evidence group. I rechecked Layton today (parfums-de-marly.com/products/layton). It declares Tetramethyl acetyloctahydronaphthalenes (Iso E Super) first after water, and also patchouli oil, vanillin and coumarin. data.js lines 106 to 109 tag no cedar_dry, patchouli or tonka_coumarin. B's own test shows mapped notes reproduce 8 of 69 audit corrections. The label tier changes tags where notes and books do not.
2. For marker families, the discount can be calibrated. Every entry with a new-format list tests the mapper. The share of mapper tags that the label confirms or rules out, family by family, sets that family's rank-1 weight. C's 8 of 12 disagreements are the first data point.
3. "The user sees one tag and cannot see its grade" does not describe A. A shows each family's source in both languages (round1_A.md, section 1).
4. B's trace objection is right, and I amend A. A marker sets a weight only if it is among the first few fragrance materials, as C proposes. A marker further down records presence only.
5. D is right that volatility classes are an unvalidated stage rule. A will use stage ratings to test that rule, as C's falsifier does, without letting ratings write tags. By D's own figures, 2,000 raters in year one bring about 18 perfumes to the 120 raters needed to test a family. A review queue can handle that number.

A also inherits B's point that Fragella's terms forbid caching payloads, so the backend should store derived tags and the Fragella id.

## Vote

1. **A.** It keeps B's full coverage through the notes tier and adds label evidence that demonstrably corrects niche tags, for about 15 more hours than B and no extra money.
2. **B.** It is the fastest and cheapest way to tag what Saudi users type, but it publishes tags that the audit and the label test show are wrong on the site's central claim.
3. **D.** It keeps the current catalogue working and its method is sound, but by its own figures it measures nothing before month 6 to 12, and a new site may never reach the traffic it needs.
4. **C.** Its tags are the most accurate and legally cleanest, but 80 hours for about 150 tagged perfumes, with most Arab-house bottles untagged and unrecommended, is the worst fit for a launch within weeks for a Saudi audience.
