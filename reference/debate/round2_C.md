# Round 2, Path C

## Rebuttal

The strongest objection comes from A, B, D and Fable: no book covers any Arab-house original, so a Saudi user who rates Khamrah, Asad and Hawas gets no profile from C at launch.

What I can answer. Arab-house bottles sold in Europe carry lists specific to the product. Boozyshop's Khamrah list declares coumarin, cinnamal, cinnamyl alcohol, eugenol and isoeugenol (boozyshop.com/products/khamrah-eau-de-parfum). That is material evidence for tonka_coumarin and spicy_warm. SkinSAFE's Yara list declares coumarin, benzyl salicylate and benzyl benzoate among about 11 items (skinsafeproducts.com/lattafa-yara-eau-de-parfum-natural-spray-3-4-fl-oz-100-ml). From 31 July 2026, any new Lattafa or Armaf launch sold in the EU must also declare Iso E Super and Galaxolide if it uses them. A cites SFDA guidance that boxes sold in Saudi Arabia carry ingredient lists, so C can accept a photo of the user's own box as a source.

A's example does not hold for its own case. A says C would tag Baccarat Rouge 540 as citrus and jasmine, but the book gives that perfume's Ambroxan, Evernyl and ethyl maltol (scent_and_chemistry_2022.txt:6216). Under my rule, a material listed beyond the first few positions records presence only and sets no weight.

What I cannot answer.

1. Some Arab-house lists are templates. oriental-style.de shows the same 22-allergen list, oakmoss and treemoss extracts included, for Yara, Khamrah and Asad: three different perfumes (oriental-style.de/en/products/ lattafa-yara-perfume-eau-de-parfum-spray, lattafa-khamrah-perfume-eau-de-perfum-spray, asad-lattafa-perfume). C must reject any list that declares nearly every permitted allergen. That removes part of the small amount of Arab-house evidence there is.
2. No label will ever show Ambroxan, Cashmeran, Norlimbanol or oud materials. For Arab-house perfumes built on woody ambers and oud accords, C will tag the tonka, spice and citrus and say nothing about the families people buy these bottles for.
3. D's point stands. Baccarat Rouge 540 contains about 12% Evernyl, yet no source reports moss, so even a large dose does not settle which family a person smells. C's material table needs expert judgment, and that judgment is not a material fact.

So C cannot launch in weeks as a useful product for a Saudi user who owns mostly Arab-house bottles. I no longer argue that C should run alone. Its evidence should be the top layer of A, and the recommender should weight tags by evidence grade.

## Vote

1. **A.** It is the only path that tags what Saudi users own at launch, lets label and literature evidence override notes, and shows each user what a claim rests on.
2. **C.** Its evidence is the only source that found errors in our tags (40 of 68 in the audit, 8 of 12 in the label test), but on its own it cannot tag most Arab-house bottles within weeks.
3. **B.** It launches fastest with the widest coverage. But it publishes the pyramid errors the product exists to correct, its own test reproduces 8 of the 69 audit corrections, and its legal standing depends on Fragella's caching terms.
4. **D.** With no ratings at launch it runs on priors for its first year and needs traffic a new site may not reach. Its shrinkage model is a later addition to A, not a source of truth.
