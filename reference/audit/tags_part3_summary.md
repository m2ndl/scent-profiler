- Named in Scent and Chemistry: 5 (Baccarat Rouge 540, Musc Ravageur, Black Aoud, Black Afgano, Tam Dao).
- No evidence in either book: 57. This includes every Parfums de Marly, Xerjoff, Initio, Mancera, Louis Vuitton, Le Labo, BDK and Amouage entry in this part.

## Where the sources contradict the marketing pyramid

- **Oud Satin Mood.** The pyramid lists oud in the heart and base. The Guide reviewer smelled none and describes an almond-biscuit gourmand beside a fruity rose.
- **Baccarat Rouge 540 Extrait.** The Extrait pyramid (bitter almond, saffron, jasmine, cedar, ambergris) has no sugar note. The Guide says the Extrait is the EDP at higher concentration, and the chemistry book gives the EDP about 3% ethyl maltol and about 12% Evernyl, an oakmoss-type material that no tag reflects. Either the separate Extrait pyramid or the Guide's claim is wrong; a human should decide which.
- **Black Aoud.** The pyramid puts rose only in the top. The chemistry book calls it a rose-dominant masculine with 19.5% patchouli oil.
- **Musc Ravageur.** The pyramid has no orris. The chemistry book lists orris among the materials used in overdose.
- **Good Girl Gone Bad.** The pyramid has no peach. The Guide files it under "peach fuzz" and calls it a peachy floral.
- **Enigma pour Homme.** The pyramid centres on tobacco and cognac. The Guide, reviewing it as Creation-E pour Homme, labels it "bergamot cardamom" and compares it to Cartier Déclaration. First confirm that the two names refer to the same perfume.
- **Danger pour Homme.** The pyramid and tags lead the drydown with leather and oakmoss. The Guide calls it "Routine woody citrus." I left the tags unchanged because the review is only three words long.
- **Not a Perfume.** The pyramid claims Cetalox alone. The reviewer doubts this and finds it muskier than Cetalox.
