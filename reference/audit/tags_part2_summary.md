## Where the sources contradict the pyramid

- **Le Male:** the pyramid's base (vanilla, tonka, sandalwood, cedar, amber) has no musk. The chemistry book says the base is 39% musk and 15% Iso E Super. These figures are for the original formula, so current batches may differ.
- **Cool Water:** the pyramid lists calone and sea water, and the opening is tagged aquatic 0.9. Neither book mentions a marine material in it. The chemistry book puts its freshness down to a doubled dose of dihydromyrcenol plus lavender materials, and dates the Calone marine trend from 1991. Someone should decide whether aquatic 0.9 stays.
- **Déclaration:** the pyramid leads with spices and gives cedar as one base note among five. The chemistry book says about 35% of the formula is Iso E Super.
- **Noir Extreme:** the pyramid promises a saffron, cardamom and citrus top and a rose-jasmine heart. The Guide says it smells like a cedar-vanilla drydown from the first spray.
- **Olympéa:** the pyramid gives green mandarin, water jasmine and ginger flower as the top. The Guide says the top is a large amber-toffee accord that fades within minutes.
- **Sì:** the pyramid has no tobacco and no powder. The Guide calls it fruity-powdery, hears tobacco and woods in the background, and does not mention the rose.
- **Lady Million:** the pyramid stresses a honey, patchouli and amber base, and the drydown has tobacco_honey 0.8. The Guide describes only peony and grapefruit in an air-freshener style. The chemistry book names a woody-amber material (Operanide) that the pyramid leaves out. The honey weighting needs a second opinion.
- **Millésime Impérial:** the pyramid puts iris and sea water in the heart. The chemistry book cites the perfume for its fruity-rosy damascone accord, and no heart tag reflects that.
- **Angel (minor):** the Guide calls the floral accord intense, but the heart has white_floral at only 0.3, below fruit and honey.
