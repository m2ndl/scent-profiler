# Material-family taxonomy: audit against *Scent and Chemistry*

Source: Ohloff, Pickenhagen, Kraft and Grau, *Scent and Chemistry: The Molecular World of Odors*, 2nd ed. (2022), read from `reference/books/scent_and_chemistry_2022.txt`. Line numbers below refer to that file.

Supporting passages are given as close paraphrases with the nearest heading and line number, not as verbatim quotes, because copyright limits apply to reproducing the book's text. The line number lets anyone read the exact wording.

Companion file: `families_proposed.json` (same keys, revised `hint_en` where recommended, a `materials` list per family, four `new_` families, and a `_note_to_families` map of 109 note words).

## How the book classifies odors

The book offers three overlapping schemes:

1. **Nine study families** (Sect. 9.3, lines 5670-5998): fruity (citrus included), aldehydic, herbal (green, agrestic, anisic, minty), marine or ozony, floral (rose, white-floral, all other flowers), spicy (cold and warm), woody (cedar, sandalwood, patchouli, vetiver, other woods, mossy), animalic (musk, civet, castoreum, indole, leathery, ambery) and balsamic (vanillic, balsamic, coumarin, powdery).
2. **Givaudan's eight commercial families**, ordered by rising molecular weight, which is also the order of evaporation from opening to drydown: fruity, green, marine, floral, spicy, woody, ambery, musky (Sect. 2.2, line 1964).
3. **Glossary notes** (Sect. 2.4, lines 2038-2192), which define each note in plain descriptors.

## Findings that apply across families

- **Specific anosmia.** The book names musks, ambery odorants and sandalwood materials as notes where many people have a partial smell blindness (2.3, line 1992), and reports many anosmias for patchouli odorants (7.6, line 4242). In a survey of 1.5 million people, Galaxolide went unsmelled about as often as androstenone, which 30 to 37% could not smell (line 1992). For these families, a neutral rating may mean the person cannot smell the material, not that they accept it.
- **Near-universal materials.** Every perfume on the market contains musks (8, Animalic Notes in Perfumery, line 5356); almost every formula contains some ionones (6.4, line 3940); Hedione is used across all families and genders (Jasmin Notes in Perfumery, line 4288); Ambrox with Isoambrox appears, at up to 0.02%, in about 40% of perfumes from the last 30 years (8.4, line 5308). These materials only separate perfumes when dosed high, so the families that contain them need dose information to work as dislike signals.
- **Stage of wear.** Citrus oils lose strength quickly, while Calone 1951 stays relatively constant (9.3, line 6000). The drydown consists of the long-lasting woody, musky and animalic materials (2.4 Glossary, Fond, line 2086).
- **Notes are not materials.** The book gives cases where the marketing notes do not name the material: Baccarat Rouge 540 is about 15% Ambroxan, 12% Evernyl (an oakmoss material) and 3% ethyl maltol (Legendary Perfumes, line 6216); "aldehyde C-14" is a peach lactone, not an aldehyde (Oakmoss Notes in Perfumery, line 4530); oud and okoumé in "loud woods" are rebuilt from cedarwood oil (Cedarwood in Perfumery, line 4440).

## Family-by-family audit

### 1. woody_amber (Woody ambers, Ambroxan-type)

- **Materials in the book.** Ambergris odorants: (-)-Ambrox/Ambroxan (the most ambery material in the book's woody-to-ambery series), Cetalox (woodier, rounder, less sharp than Ambrox), Isoambrox, (-)-alpha-ambrinol, ambra aldehyde, Ambrinal, Amberketal, Superambrox (extremely powerful). High-impact woody-ambers: Ambrocenide (intense woody, ambery; extremely low threshold), Ysamber K, Karanal (powerful, radiant, hot-tar aspects; discontinued), Okoumal (woody-ambery with musky, tobacco aspects), Ambermax (exceptionally intense ambery-woody), Symroxane, Ambramone, Acetarolle, Belambre, Trisamber (ambery, animalic), Amber Xtreme (dry, ambery-woody), Timberol and Dextro Norlimbanol (powdery, woody, animalic, almost sweaty or urinous). The ambery glossary note combines woody, incense-like, earthy, camphoraceous, tobacco and musk facets with an ocean smell (line 2050).
- **Boundary.** Right. The book shows a continuous series from Ambrox to woody, almost spicy materials (2.2, line 1970), so the line against cedar_dry is a convention (see family 11). The Timberol type adds a sweaty, urinous note that Ambrox lacks (lines 2506, 2538), so it may divide people differently. The word "amber" is shared with amber_resin, but the book treats the ambery note (ambergris) as distinct from the oriental "amber" accord (lines 2054, 2164).
- **Hint.** The current hint's claims that this base is in "most" designer scents and is "the usual cause" of a chemical drydown are not in the book. Revised: "Dry, radiant, slightly sharp 'amberwood' base (Ambroxan and related, stronger synthetics) found in many modern scents; it lasts for hours, and some people can barely smell it."
- **Support (paraphrase).** Ambrox with Isoambrox is used at up to 0.02% in about 40% of perfumes created in the past 30 years (8.4 Ambergris, line 5308). Ambery odorants are among the notes with frequent specific anosmia (2.3 Quantitative Odor Perception, line 1992).

### 2. white_musk (Clean white musks)

- **Materials in the book.** Polycyclic musks: Galaxolide (built the musk accords that recall clean, hot-ironed linen; heavy use in laundry care), Tonalide/Fixolide, Celestolide, Phantolide. Linear musks: Helvetolide (musky-floral, fruity, pear-type), Romandolide, Serenolide, Sylkolide, Edenolide (fruity, apple). Macrocyclic: Habanolide/Globalide (metallic, hot-iron), ethylene brassylate (the standard low-cost macrocyclic musk; no descriptor given). Nitro: Musk xylene (penetratingly sweet; soaps and detergents; withdrawn).
- **Boundary.** Too narrow as the only musk family. The book's benchmark musk, muscone, is warm, sensual, sweet-powdery and skin-like (line 2122), which is a different smell from the linen accords, and perfumers mix musk classes because many people are anosmic to one class (line 5360). Warm skin musks (muscone, Muscenone, Nirvanolide, Musk ketone, Cashmeran) have no family now; see new_skin_musk. Overlap with aldehydes: the book uses the ironed-laundry comparison for both (lines 2046, 5358); stage separates them, since aldehydes appear in the opening.
- **Hint.** Revised to fit the narrower family and the anosmia evidence: "Clean-laundry musk: soft, slightly soapy, like freshly ironed cotton; many people cannot smell one or more of these musks."
- **Support (paraphrase).** Helvetolide set the trend for white musk accords, paired with the metallic, hot-iron musk of Habanolide, giving a soft cotton-and-linen impression (8.3 Nonnatural Musks, line 5274).

### 3. vanilla_gourmand (Vanilla and sugar)

- **Materials in the book.** Vanilla absolute Bourbon, vanillin (most typical sweet vanilla odor), ethyl vanillin; caramel odorants: maltol, ethyl maltol (caramel, candy floss), Furaneol, Cyclotene (maple syrup), Sotolone.
- **Boundary.** Right for dislike inference, though it crosses two book classes: vanillin is balsamic and ethyl maltol is listed as fruity (9.3), and caramel odorants form their own structural class (3.9). Overlaps fruity_sweet (ethyl maltol in the cherry and strawberry accords, 9.4), amber_resin (vanilla is part of the amber accord) and coffee_gourmand (bitter chocolate is a gourmand theme).
- **Hint.** Keep.
- **Support (paraphrase).** Caramel odorants underlie the gourmand perfumes that followed Angel, which brought crème caramel, bitter chocolate, cotton candy and candied fruit into perfumery (3.9 Bifunctional Units: Muguet and Caramel Odorants, line 2662).

### 4. tonka_coumarin (Tonka and hay)

- **Materials in the book.** Coumarin (freshly mown hay, marzipan), tonka bean absolute.
- **Boundary.** Right. The book files coumarin notes in the balsamic family beside vanilla (line 5982). Overlaps lavender_aromatic and oakmoss_chypre through the fougère accord (lavender, oakmoss, coumarin; line 1738), and labdanum has coumarin-like parts (7.17).
- **Hint.** Keep. "Softer than vanilla" is not stated in the book but does not conflict with it.
- **Support (paraphrase).** Coumarin, with its note of freshly mown hay and marzipan, is the smelling principle of tonka beans (1.2 Chemical Discoveries and Modern Perfumery, line 1738).

### 5. oud_smoky (Oud, smoky or medicinal)

- **Materials in the book.** No oud accord is described by odor. The book says agarwood oil is not on the regular palette and that "loud woods" are reconstituted with cedarwood oil (line 4440); rotundone, a dry peppery odorant, is important for the scent of agarwood (line 4568); Akigalawood is a patchouli fraction enriched in agarwood-type components (line 4256).
- **Boundary.** Not verifiable from this book. What the book does show is that Western oud accords are built from cedarwood and peppery-woody materials, so this family shares materials with cedar_dry and new_fresh_spice. "Medicinal" has no support in the book's oud passages.
- **Hint.** Keep (no evidence for or against).
- **Support (paraphrase).** Neither agarwood nor okoumé oil is on the regular palette, so both are reconstituted with cedarwood oil (Cedarwood in Perfumery, line 4440).

### 6. oud_animalic (Oud, animalic)

- **Materials in the book.** Oud oil, listed once, inside the animalic family (9.3, line 5976); castoreum, used in Arabian oud-wood themes (line 5364).
- **Boundary.** Not verifiable as a separate family; the placement of oud oil among animalic materials is consistent with the name. Overlaps animalic and leather_smoky through castoreum.
- **Hint.** Keep. "Barnyard" is not in the book.
- **Support (paraphrase).** Castoreum is an ingredient in Arabian oud-wood themes that luxury niche houses made popular (Animalic Notes in Perfumery, line 5364).

### 7. oakmoss_chypre (Oakmoss)

- **Materials in the book.** Oakmoss absolute (intense mossy-woody, humid, with phenolic, smoky, animalic side notes), tree moss, Orcinyl 3 (woody, mossy, phenolic), methyl and ethyl everninate (dusty, earthy, mossy), 3-methoxy-2,5-dimethylphenol, Evernyl/Veramoss (reproduces oakmoss closely; black-ink impression in Legend, line 3946). Since the 2009 IFRA amendment oakmoss extracts are limited to 0.1% in fine fragrance, so most modern mossy bases rely on Evernyl (7.18).
- **Boundary.** Right. Evernyl now also appears outside chypres (about 12% in Baccarat Rouge 540, line 6216), so the family will often be present without any moss note listed.
- **Hint.** "Green" has no support; "bitter" does (the classic chypre base is described as somewhat bitter, line 1768). Revised: "Damp, earthy tree moss with a faintly bitter, inky note; the base of classic chypre and fougère scents."
- **Support (paraphrase).** Oakmoss extracts smell intensely mossy-woody with a humid tone and phenolic, smoky and animalic side notes (7.18 Oakmoss, line 4514).

### 8. patchouli (Patchouli)

- **Materials in the book.** Patchouli oil (woody, balsamic; camphoraceous, woody, earthy; slightly floral), (-)-patchoulol (earthy, slightly camphoraceous, powdery cellar note), nor-patchoulenol (rooty cellar note like potatoes), cypriol oil. Off-notes: patchouli pyridines (nauseating, rooty).
- **Boundary.** Right; a woody subfamily in the book. Many people are anosmic to some patchouli odorants (line 4242). Overlaps vanilla_gourmand (patchouli counterbalances sugar in Angel-type gourmands, line 4260) and oakmoss_chypre.
- **Hint.** Keep.
- **Support (paraphrase).** Patchouli's scent balances three attributes, camphoraceous, woody and earthy, with slight floral facets (7.6 Patchouli Oil, line 4232).

### 9. vetiver (Vetiver)

- **Materials in the book.** Vetiver oil (suave, sweet woody-earthy with grapefruit and rhubarb nuances; Bourbon grade rosy and sulfury; Haiti grade jute and roasted peanuts; Java and China grades harsh and smoky), vetiveryl acetate (less earthy, drier, toward sandalwood), khusimone, 3-epi-zizaen-2-one (the smelling principle: woody-ambery and transparent, like Iso E Super).
- **Boundary.** Right. Its key odorant resembles Iso E Super (line 4414), so light vetivers can read as cedar_dry.
- **Hint.** "Grassy" is not supported. Revised: "Earthy, rooty wood with a grapefruit-like freshness; some grades smell smoky."
- **Support (paraphrase).** Vetiver oil has a suave, sweet woody-earthy odor with grapefruit and rhubarb nuances (7.13 Vetiver Oil, line 4406).

### 10. sandalwood_creamy (Creamy sandalwood)

- **Materials in the book.** Sandalwood oil (woody-milky; creamy-lactonic; smoky dryness like cedar; skin and rose petals; urinous-animalic, musky facets), (Z)-alpha-santalol, (Z)-beta-santalol (main odor vector); synthetics Javanol (most intense), Polysantol, Ebanol, Sandalore, Sandranol, Sandolen. Most sandalwood accords are now synthetic (line 4462).
- **Boundary.** Right. Sandalwood materials are a named specific-anosmia group (line 1992).
- **Hint.** "Milky, soft wood" is vague. Revised: "Soft, milky-creamy wood with a faint skin-like note; mostly synthetic today, and some people barely smell it."
- **Support (paraphrase).** Natural sandalwood oil is milky and creamy-lactonic unlike any other wood, and recalls skin and rose petals (Sandalwood Oil in Perfumery, line 4466).

### 11. cedar_dry (Dry cedar / Iso E Super)

- **Materials in the book.** Cedarwood oils (Virginia, Texas, Atlas), (+)-alpha-cedrol (balsamic, woody), cedryl acetate (woody, powdery), Vertofix Coeur (intense warm woody with a musky undertone, also called woody-ambery), Cedramber (fine cedarwood with ambery notes), Iso E Super and its key odorant arborone (transparent woody, ambery; structurally related to the ionones), Georgywood, guaiac wood oil (no descriptor).
- **Boundary.** Overlaps woody_amber. The book uses "woody, ambery" for Vertofix, Cedramber and Iso E Super as well as for Ambrocenide and Karanal, and presents woody and ambery as one continuous series (line 1970). Keep both families but define the line explicitly: cedar_dry holds cedarwood oils, cedrol derivatives and the soft, transparent Iso E Super type; woody_amber holds ambergris odorants and the high-impact woody-ambers. Iso E Super is dosed from 18% up to 100% in the perfumes the book lists (line 3948); if ratings show it dividing people apart from cedar, it should become its own family.
- **Hint.** Keep: pencil shavings comes from the book's woody note (line 2192) and "transparent" from its Iso E Super descriptor.
- **Support (paraphrase).** The woody note recalls freshly cut wood, pencil shavings and sawdust (2.4 Glossary, Woody Note, line 2192); Iso E Super has a transparent woody, ambery odor (6.4, line 3948).

### 12. leather_smoky (Leather and birch smoke)

- **Materials in the book.** 6-Isobutylquinoline (very powerful, long-lasting; warm, sweet, earthy-mossy leather at low concentration), birch-tar oil, cade oil, castoreum (leathery, animalic), labdanum (smoky, leathery phenols), styrax oil. Ionones are used to soften the animalic, smoky and phenolic side of suede (line 3942).
- **Boundary.** Right, with overlaps: castoreum with animalic, labdanum with amber_resin, and saffron, which the glossary places inside the leathery note (line 2114).
- **Hint.** "Tarry, smoky, leathery" defines leather by itself. Revised: "Tanned leather and birch-tar smoke: smoky, tarry and sometimes harsh or animal-like."
- **Support (paraphrase).** The leathery note suggests freshly tanned leather, Moroccan saffron leather or Russian leather, and includes birch-tar oil and isobutylquinoline (2.4 Glossary, Leathery Note, line 2114).

### 13. incense_resin (Incense)

- **Materials in the book.** Olibanum oil and resin (intense balsamic; resinous acids), trans-olibanic acids (extremely potent, church-like olibanum smell), Mystikal (resinous incense of humid tone), myrrh oil (warm, leathery-balsamic constituents), opoponax resinoid (incense-like).
- **Boundary.** Right. The book files olibanum oil in its woody family (9.3) but also says an archetypal frankincense perfume would not be called oriental if incense had its own family (line 4508), which supports keeping it separate. Overlaps amber_resin (resins) and cedar_dry (the Comme des Garçons incense series uses 51-55% Iso E Super, line 3948).
- **Hint.** Keep.
- **Support (paraphrase).** The more powerful trans-olibanic acid has an extremely potent, church-like olibanum smell (7.19 Incense and Myrrh, line 4536).

### 14. amber_resin (Resinous amber)

- **Materials in the book.** Labdanum resin, absolute and oil (tenacious woody-ambery, leathery; contains Ambrox; smoky-leathery phenols; coumarin-like and hay-like parts; raspberry-ketone fruitiness), benzoin resin. The balsamic note is sweet, soft and warm, with cinnamon, vanilla and chocolate elements (line 2058).
- **Boundary.** Right as an accord family (the classic amber base), but it spans book classes: labdanum is listed with leathery notes and benzoin with balsamic notes (9.3). Overlaps vanilla_gourmand, tonka_coumarin, leather_smoky and, through the Ambrox in labdanum, woody_amber. The book notes that "oriental" is being renamed "amber" in perfumery (line 2164), so "amber" in a note list usually means this accord.
- **Hint.** Keep.
- **Support (paraphrase).** In Balmain's Ambre Gris the ambergris facets are combined with a labdanum, benzoin, vanilla and tonka accord (Animalic Notes in Perfumery, line 5362).

### 15. aldehydes (Aldehydes)

- **Materials in the book.** Aldehyde C-10 (decanal; recalls oranges), C-11, C-12 lauric (dodecanal; rather floral), C-12 MNA (boosts and freshens florals), octanal. Descriptors: fatty-sweaty to pungent-soapy, metallic-waxy, hot-ironed laundry (line 2046); fatty, citrusy, mineral (line 5724).
- **Boundary.** Right. Misleading note words: "aldehyde C-14" (gamma-undecalactone, peach), C-16 (strawberry) and C-18 belong to fruity_sweet despite the name (line 4530; 9.3 fruity list). Overlaps citrus_fresh (decanal and dodecanal occur in citrus oils) and white_musk (laundry comparison).
- **Hint.** "Old-fashioned" is not in the book. Revised: "Waxy, soapy, slightly metallic sparkle at the start of classic floral perfumes (the Chanel No. 5 style), often compared to hot-ironed laundry."
- **Support (paraphrase).** The aldehydic note runs from fatty-sweaty to pungent-soapy, with metallic-waxy facets that recall hot-ironed laundry (2.4 Glossary, Aldehydic Note, line 2046).

### 16. white_floral (White florals)

- **Materials in the book.** Jasmine absolutes (grandiflorum, sambac), benzyl acetate, 1H-indole (jasmine only at high dilution; also animalic), cis-jasmone, methyl jasmonate, Hedione, Hedione HC and Paradisone (transparent floral-jasminic with a lemony softness), Jasmolactone (jasmine with oily, coconut-lactonic facets), tuberose absolute and the tuberose accord (benzyl salicylate, delta-decalactone, methyl anthranilate, eugenol, indole), neroli oil (fresh floral with a dried-hay undertone), orange flower absolute (more jasminic), ylang-ylang oil.
- **Boundary.** Right core, three caveats. Hedione is used everywhere, so it does not mark this family (line 4288). Indole is also listed in the book's animalic family (line 5946). Neroli and orange blossom also belong to the hesperidic family (line 2110). Other flowers, notably lily of the valley, have no family now; see new_muguet_floral.
- **Hint.** "Indolic" is jargon. Revised: "Heavy, sweet jasmine, tuberose and orange-blossom scent; some versions have a faint animal-like note from indole."
- **Support (paraphrase).** In the Eternity formula the book glosses indole as jasmine and animalic in character (Legendary Perfumes, line 6182); indole gives an exotic jasmine note only when highly diluted (7.3, line 4186).

### 17. rose (Rose)

- **Materials in the book.** Rose oil and absolute, 2-phenylethanol, citronellol, geraniol, nerol, rose oxide (diffusive floral-green top note), nerol oxide, rose furan (fresh citrusy part), beta-damascenone and beta-damascone (fruity-floral: plum, rose, raisins, blackcurrant, prunes), honey aspects from damascenone with phenethyl esters, geranium oil (the "male rose": less sweet, more green, herbal and citrusy).
- **Boundary.** Too broad. It holds two odor types the book separates: the fresh petal and green rose alcohols, and the damascones, which since Poison (1985) are used as main notes far outside rose themes (line 3944). See new_damascone_dried_fruit. Overlaps tobacco_honey through damascenone.
- **Hint.** "Fresh or jammy rose" is vague. Revised: "Rose petals, from fresh, green and lemony to deep, honeyed and jammy; geranium, a greener rose, belongs here too."
- **Support (paraphrase).** The two rose ketones share a narcotic fruity-floral odor recalling plum, rose, raisins, blackcurrant and prunes (7.8 Rose Oil, line 4302); geranium is described as the male rose of perfumery (line 4326).

### 18. iris_powdery (Iris and powder)

- **Materials in the book.** Orris butter, orris root absolute, orris resinoid (powdery, buttery), alpha-irone (the orris principle), alpha- and beta-ionone (sweet, woody-violet, powdery side), Isoraldeine and Raldeine (methyl ionones). Powdery notes the book files as balsamic: heliotropin (sweet, spicy, warm floral heliotrope), anisaldehyde.
- **Boundary.** Right. The book treats violet and orris as one family (6.1 heading; line 3940). Because ionones are in almost every formula, only ionone-heavy perfumes (the Grojsman accord, violet and orris soliflores) will signal this family. Powderiness also comes from musks and Timberol.
- **Hint.** Keep: the book describes the Grojsman accord as cosmetic in style and cites a violet-orris perfume built as a lipstick homage (lines 3940-3942).
- **Support (paraphrase).** Cheap synthetic ionones made the violet smell common from toilet soap to fabric softener, leaving orris and alpha-irone as the luxury members of the family (6.4 Ionone-Type Materials in Perfumery, line 3940).

### 19. citrus_fresh (Citrus)

- **Materials in the book.** Bergamot, lemon, orange, bitter orange, mandarin, grapefruit, lime, yuzu oils; citral, limonene, linalyl acetate (main odor vector of bergamot). The hesperidic family extends to petitgrain and neroli.
- **Boundary.** Right. Overlaps white_floral (neroli, orange blossom) and aldehydes (decanal recalls oranges).
- **Hint.** Keep: citrus oils lose strength quickly (line 6000).
- **Support (paraphrase).** Hesperidic materials are light, radiant and refreshing and are linked with freshness and cleanliness (2.4 Glossary, Hesperidic Note, line 2110).

### 20. aquatic_marine (Aquatic / marine)

- **Materials in the book.** Calone 1951 (salty, marine-specific; stays relatively constant on the blotter), seaweed absolute, Helional/Tropional (listed in the marine family), Azurone (marine and floral). The marine glossary note runs from salty seaweed to fresh, ozonic sea breeze (line 2118).
- **Boundary.** Right. Overlaps green_herbal (watery-cucumber nonadienal links green to marine, line 1964) and woody_amber (ambery materials have a marine side, line 1964).
- **Hint.** "Melon" is not in the book; saltiness and persistence are. Revised: "Salty sea air and watery freshness (Calone); unlike citrus, it does not fade quickly."
- **Support (paraphrase).** Kenzo pour Homme introduced the salty, marine-specific smell of Calone 1951 (Legendary Perfumes, line 6224); Calone stays fairly constant as it evaporates (9.3, line 6000).

### 21. fruity_sweet (Sweet fruit)

- **Materials in the book.** Gamma-undecalactone ("aldehyde C-14", ripe peach), gamma-octalactone, aldehyde C-16 (strawberry accord), aldehyde C-18, raspberry ketone, ethyl methyl butyrate and Agrumex (apple), hexyl acetate (pear), amyl acetate, benzaldehyde (cherry and bitter almond), Oxane, cassis base and cassis bud absolute (blackcurrant), Dynascone (pineapple-like, green), davana oil.
- **Boundary.** Right. Overlaps vanilla_gourmand (ethyl maltol), new_damascone_dried_fruit (apple, plum), white_musk (Helvetolide is pear-like), iris_powdery (the raspberry accord uses alpha-ionone) and tonka_coumarin (almond).
- **Hint.** The list omits peach and pear, which are the notes of the book's main fruity materials. Revised: "Ripe or candied fruit: peach, pear, apple, berries, pineapple, cherry-almond and coconut."
- **Support (paraphrase).** Gamma-undecalactone gives a powerful, long-lasting fruity odor very close to ripe peaches (1.2, line 1768).

### 22. spicy_warm (Warm spices)

- **Materials in the book.** Warm spices: cinnamon bark oil, cinnamaldehyde, cinnamic alcohol, clove bud oil, eugenol (clove; no known smell blindness), nutmeg oil, cumin oil. The hint also lists cardamom and pepper, which the book classes as cold spices.
- **Boundary.** Wrong: the family name says warm but the hint mixes the book's two spice groups. Split out new_fresh_spice.
- **Hint.** Revised: "Sweet, warm kitchen spices: cinnamon, clove, nutmeg."
- **Support (paraphrase).** The book divides its spicy family into cold spices (black and pink pepper, ginger, cardamom) and warm spices (clove, cinnamon) (9.3, line 5884).

### 23. lavender_aromatic (Lavender and aromatics)

- **Materials in the book.** Lavender, lavandin and spike oils (spike is camphor-rich), linalool, linalyl acetate, lavandulyl acetate, clary sage oil (herbaceous, sweet, lavender-like with ambergris and tobacco undertones), rosemary, thyme, marjoram, eucalyptus (agrestic), peppermint, spearmint, menthol, carvone (minty), anise, anethole, tarragon, estragol (anisic), camphor.
- **Boundary.** Too narrow. The book's agrestic note and herbal family put lavender with rosemary, sage, thyme, eucalyptus and mint (line 2042; 9.3), and camphor is its benchmark for camphoraceous odors (line 1976). The current taxonomy puts sage and mint in green_herbal instead. Move them here. Overlaps tonka_coumarin and oakmoss_chypre through fougère.
- **Hint.** "Barbershop, herbal-clean" is vague. Revised: "Lavender and dry herbs such as rosemary, sage, thyme and mint: fresh and slightly camphor-like, the smell of classic men's fougère and lavender soap."
- **Support (paraphrase).** Agrestic notes range from minty-camphoraceous to herbaceous-floral and include rosemary, lavender, marjoram, eucalyptus and clary sage (2.4 Glossary, Agrestic Note, line 2042); lavender has a dated image linked to eye pillows and Yardley soap (line 4214).

### 24. green_herbal (Green and herbal)

- **Materials in the book.** Galbanum oil (intense green leaves over a woody, balsamic undertone), leaf alcohol ((3Z)-hex-3-en-1-ol), cis-3-hexenyl acetate, Cyclal C/Ligustral, styralyl acetate, violet leaf absolute (green shade), Undecavertol, Dynascone, (2E,6Z)-nona-2,6-dienal (green, watery cucumber), 2-isobutyl-3-methoxypyrazine (green bell pepper), Magnolan, phenylacetaldehyde.
- **Boundary.** Too broad and overlapping. Its hint names sage and mint (agrestic and minty in the book) and juniper (a spice oil and a coniferous note in the book, lines 2074, 5902). Narrow it to green notes.
- **Hint.** Revised: "Sharp green smell of cut grass, crushed leaves and stems, and galbanum; can be slightly bitter."
- **Support (paraphrase).** The book names three principal green notes: green apple, freshly cut grass and galbanum (2.4 Glossary, Green Note, line 2098); vetiveryl acetate softens the bitterness of crushed leaves and stems in Bel Respiro (line 4426).

### 25. animalic (Animalic)

- **Materials in the book.** Civet and civetone (musk with animalic, sweet and fecal facets; sweet civet character now seen as old-fashioned), castoreum (leathery, animalic; nicotine-like basic components; polarizing, dirty), musk tincture and muscopyridine (urinous), 1H-indole, beeswax absolute. Only synthetic reconstitutions are used now.
- **Boundary.** Right; the website already splits the book's broad animalic family (which also holds musk, leather and ambery notes). Overlaps new_skin_musk ("skin" is the book's musk descriptor, line 2122), leather_smoky and oud_animalic (castoreum), and white_floral (indole).
- **Hint.** "Skin" belongs to the musk descriptor. Revised: "Animal-gland smells (civet, castoreum, now made synthetically): warm fur with a slightly dirty, fecal or leathery note."
- **Support (paraphrase).** Castoreum notes divide people because they often have a dirty touch (Animalic Notes in Perfumery, line 5364); civetone has animalic, sweet and fecal facets (3.7, line 2538).

### 26. tobacco_honey (Tobacco and honey)

- **Materials in the book.** Tobacco odorants: megastigmatrienones (tobacco-like, woody, balsamic; key to burley tobacco), beta-damascenone and beta-damascone (contribute to burley tobacco), Amberketal isomers (tobacco flavor constituents). Honey appears only as an aspect of rose oil (damascenone with phenethyl esters) and in the sweet glossary note (line 2176); beeswax absolute is filed as animalic.
- **Boundary.** Weakly supported pairing. The book has a tobacco note but no honey class, and the materials that link tobacco to honey are the damascones. Keep, but expect overlap with new_damascone_dried_fruit and rose.
- **Hint.** Revised: "Sweet pipe or cigar tobacco leaf, often with dried-fruit, hay or honey tones."
- **Support (paraphrase).** The tobacco note spans fresh sauced pipe tobacco, cigar tobacco and cold ashtrays (2.4 Glossary, Tobacco Note, line 2180); a small structural change turns beta-ionone's violet smell into a dried-fruit, tobacco note (3.2, line 2460).

### 27. coffee_gourmand (Coffee and cacao)

- **Materials in the book.** 2-Furfurylthiol (freshly brewed coffee at low levels), guaiacol (intense smoky; roasted coffee), pyrazines (tetramethylpyrazine; 3-ethyl-2,5-dimethylpyrazine, earthy and roasty; 2-ethyl-3,5-dimethylpyrazine, roasted, recalling coffee and cocoa), 2,3,5-trimethylpyrazine in the Chocovan base (bittersweet, nutty chocolate).
- **Boundary.** Right. Overlaps vanilla_gourmand (bitter chocolate), amber_resin (chocolate is part of the balsamic note) and castoreum (which contains coffee pyrazines).
- **Hint.** "Roasted, bitter-sweet" is vague. Revised: "Roasted coffee and dark cocoa: toasty and bitter-sweet rather than sugary."
- **Support (paraphrase).** Certain pyrazines in black pepper oil give roasted notes recalling coffee and cocoa (7.20, line 4564); furfurylthiol at low levels smells of freshly brewed coffee (2.3, line 2012).

### 28. saffron_leathery (Saffron)

- **Materials in the book.** Saffron oil only, listed in the spicy family (line 5908). The glossary places saffron inside the leathery note (line 2114), and a rose perfume is described as spicy-metallic with saffron and dates (line 4308). No saffron synthetics are described.
- **Boundary.** Not verifiable beyond saffron oil; overlaps leather_smoky and the spice families.
- **Hint.** Keep. "Leathery" and "metallic" have some support; "medicinal" has none, but the book does not contradict it.
- **Support (paraphrase).** The leathery note includes Moroccan saffron leather (2.4 Glossary, Leathery Note, line 2114).

## Missing families

Proposed (added to `families_proposed.json` with `new_` keys):

- **new_muguet_floral (lily of the valley).** The book names muguet as the main odor family built on bifunctional odorants (3.9, line 2620) and says hydroxycitronellal was a common part of most floral perfumes (line 2622). Materials: hydroxycitronellal, Hydroxycitral, Lyral (banned from EU cosmetics in 2011, line 2626), Lilial, Bourgeonal, Cyclamen aldehyde, Florhydral (fresh, aquatic muguet, line 2742), Florol, cis-Mayol, Super Muguet. No current family covers these: white_floral is jasmine-type and rose is rose-type. Hint: "Fresh, sweet lily-of-the-valley floral, light and clean rather than heavy."
- **new_damascone_dried_fruit (rose ketones).** Distinct odor (plum, raisin, blackcurrant, prune, apple; line 4302), used as main notes since Poison (line 3944) and in men's scents through Pomarose (apple, rose, dried fruit: 1 Million, Legend, Be Delicious for Men; lines 3944-3946). Materials: beta-damascenone, alpha-, beta- and delta-damascone, isodamascone, Pomarose. Moderate confidence: test whether ratings of damascone-heavy perfumes move together before splitting it from rose. Hint: "Jammy dried-fruit smell of plum, raisin and apple with a rosy note, common in Poison-style florals and many modern men's scents."
- **new_fresh_spice (pepper, cardamom).** The book's own spice family has a cold group (line 5884). Black pepper oil is intense, spicy and peppery; pink pepper oil is milder, sweeter and creamier and recalls juniper (line 4558); rotundone gives a dry peppery note at an extremely low threshold (line 4568). Materials: black and pink pepper oils, rotundone, rotundol, Akigalawood, cardamom oil, ginger oil, juniper berry oil. Hint: "Dry, peppery spices: black and pink pepper, cardamom, ginger; sharp and fresh rather than sweet."
- **new_skin_musk (warm skin musks).** The book's musk benchmark is warm, sensual, sweet-powdery and skin-like (line 2122), unlike the linen-type white musks (line 5274), and anosmia differs by musk class (line 5360). Materials: muscone, muscenone, Nirvanolide (sweet, powdery, slightly animalic), Polvolide, Exaltolide, Exaltone, ambrettolide, Velvione/Ambrettone, Globanone, Aurelione (warm, powdery, tobacco-ambery), Cosmone, Moxalone, Nebulone, Cashmeran (musky-powdery, woody-ambery, halfway between wood and musk, line 5270), Musk ketone and Musk ambrette (withdrawn nitro musks). Caveat: every perfume contains musk (line 5356), so only musk-forward perfumes will signal it. Hint: "Warm, soft, powdery musk that smells like skin, sometimes faintly animal-like."

Considered and not proposed:

- **Violet (ionone) versus iris.** The book treats violet and orris as one family (6.1 heading; line 3940). Keep iris_powdery whole.
- **Lactonic or milky.** The book has no such class. Lactones are spread across fruity (peach, the C-18 material), white floral (tuberose's delta-decalactone, Jasmolactone) and sandalwood (creamy-lactonic). Revisit only if ratings show milky perfumes grouping.
- **Aromatic camphoraceous.** Supported, but as a boundary change: the agrestic class (line 2042) and the camphor benchmark (line 1976) are folded into lavender_aromatic rather than added as a new family.
- **Sulfurous or fruity thiols.** The book covers thiols as structure-odor and flavor chemistry (grapefruit juice on dilution and blackcurrant, line 2428; coffee, line 2012; sweat, line 2020), not as a perfumery family. Cassis and Oxane are listed in its fruity family.
- **Anisic (anise, licorice).** A subgroup of the book's herbal family; folded into lavender_aromatic.
- **Coniferous (pine, fir).** A glossary note exists (line 2074) but the book names no perfumery materials for it beyond juniper and cedar leaf.
- **Powdery as its own family.** Powderiness comes from several classes (heliotropin and anisaldehyde in balsamic, orris, musks, Timberol); no single material class.
- **Caramel apart from vanilla.** Caramel odorants are a distinct structural class (3.9), but both read as sugary; keep together unless ratings split them.

## Merges or splits recommended

- Split spicy_warm into warm spices (cinnamon, clove, nutmeg) and new_fresh_spice: the book's spice family already divides cold from warm (line 5884).
- Split the damascones out of rose into new_damascone_dried_fruit: distinct odor and use as main notes since 1985 (lines 3944, 4302).
- Split warm skin musks out of white_musk into new_skin_musk: different descriptors and class-specific anosmia (lines 2122, 5274, 5360).
- Move sage, mint and the other agrestic herbs from green_herbal to lavender_aromatic, and juniper to new_fresh_spice: follows the book's agrestic and spice lists (lines 2042, 5902).
- Keep cedar_dry and woody_amber separate, with the explicit line given under family 11: the book shows a continuum, not a class boundary (line 1970).
- Keep amber_resin and woody_amber separate: the book treats the ambery note and the amber (oriental) accord as different things (lines 2054, 2164).
- No merge or split of oud_smoky and oud_animalic on this book's evidence: it does not describe oud's odor.
- Do not merge saffron_leathery into leather_smoky yet: the book puts saffron in both the leathery note and the spice family but names no material beyond saffron oil.
